Fx R²=0.914 / Fy R²=0.944, outperforming the single-layer baseline; the deep layer tracks whole-body translation and rotation while the shallow layer tracks contact-surface slip, and the **transient difference in total pressure response between the two layers** is most sensitive for slip detection. ### Robotics

**① Touch goes from "one sensor" to "a whole infrastructure" in a single day** —— [N0-Foundation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29601) · [DexTacWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24976) · [Dual-layer electronic skin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22493) · [Tactile-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24385) · [Touch vs vision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24068)

- **Analysis**: Five items landing on the same day, but at completely different levels, and together they form a chain from hardware to representation to evaluation. The hardware layer (dual-layer FSR) solves the problem that "a single layer can only measure normal force" — instead of adding more transducers, it converts shear loads into an **inter-layer center-of-pressure offset**, a geometric decoupling trick; the representation layer (Tactile-JEPA) addresses the fact that "the sensing elements of electronic skins are sparse and irregularly arranged, so directly applying visual self-supervision is not optimal" — hence the masking must be guided by the **sensor connectivity graph** rather than a pixel grid; the data layer (N0-Foundation) uses 30,000 hours of synchronized data plus a tactile version of UMI to flatten the collection barrier, and deliberately pushes the data distribution toward deformable objects, precision assembly, and fine force control — the areas where vision falls short; the model layer (DexTacWAM) delivers the sharpest conclusion — **treating touch as a conditional input is not enough; contact evolution must be treated as a world state to be predicted** — and removing that drops four tasks from 74.7 to 26.6; the control group (touch vs vision) answers "is it worth paying for this" — in cluttered scenes, vision-only gets 14/25 while adding the wrench and tactile arrays gets 24/25. (Source nature: all **Author's claim**; the dual-layer skin's R² and the touch-vs-vision success counts are self-reported experimental values from the papers)

**② "How actions are expressed" is being upgraded from an engineering detail to a consistency risk** —— [World model invariance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23252) · [vla.cpp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08094)

- **Analysis**: The former is the single most worth-reading paper of the day. Training with absolute joint targets versus relative increments used to be treated as a harmless implementation choice, yet it **silently determines whether an action-conditioned world model is usable**: with a different encoding of the same trajectory, retrieval drops 2.6–13.4×, action selection 53%→15%, and on PushT the two beliefs about the same future are nearly orthogonal (cos=0.067) — showing the predictor is not degrading but answering a different question. More weighty, it provides a **discrimination framework**: first test whether the two encodings can reconstruct each other (R²=0.996, no information lost), then distinguish "effective reparameterization / lossy summary / sensor substitution", and on that basis it rejects three of the four axes it had itself proposed. The fix (averaging over targets) also leaves a loose end: worst-case consistency of 0.78, reaching 0.995 only with an inconsistency penalty, and on PushT averaging alone doesn't fix it. vla.cpp offers a cognate piece of engineering evidence: in the SmolVLA case, **positional-index precision directly correlates with gripper commands and task success rates**, so the authors stress that "numerical checks on fixed inputs must be accompanied by rollout evaluation". (Source nature: all **Author's claim**, preprints)

**③ Simulation and actuators are both "rebuilt from the demand side" in the same week** —— [Uranus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24815) · [Differentiable simulation framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07107) · [Gear actuator co-design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22795)

- **Analysis**: All three are dismantling the same old structure. On simulation, Uranus simply abandons hand-built modeling, using "autoregressive diffusion conditioned on joint trajectories" to generate frames directly at 24 FPS streaming; the differentiable simulation framework goes the other way — making derivatives extremely fast (5 microseconds for 7 DoF, 95 microseconds for 36 DoF, more than two orders of magnitude faster) to feed reinforcement learning and trajectory optimization. On actuators, the gear-actuator paper points out that optimizing by **component-level metrics** such as torque density/power density does not necessarily translate into a better robot at the task level, switching instead to task-oriented system-level co-design and delivering hardware: 35.7 N·m/kg, about 60% higher and 18.6% lighter than an in-service commercial joint, and a 12.0% higher jump on the bench under identical conditions at twice the rated torque. (Source nature: all **Author's claim**; the actuator density and bench comparisons are self-reported **Measured** values from the paper)

**④ A counter-intuitive evaluation lesson** —— [Six-condition framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22299)

- **Analysis**: This paper offers no method, only **failure localization**. After splitting "diagnosis and adaptation when robots meet unfamiliar physical conditions" into six ordered conditions, the conclusion is: the whole chain holds in controlled environments, and after transfer to unseen regimes it breaks at **evidence use** — the information is present in the trajectories (a linear model using only trajectory increments can recover the correct choice), yet the frozen decoder does not change its decisions at all, dropping such decisions to chance level, while decisions solvable with low-cost evidence remain correct. The danger is that this divergence is **masked by overall accuracy**. (Source nature: **Author's claim**, preprint)

### Embedded

**① On the deployment side, the center of gravity moves from "model compression" to "building your own runtime"** —— [vla.cpp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08094) · [vla.simd](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24274) · [FoldQuantVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24433)

- **Analysis**: Three items with different landing points but the same direction — no longer getting to the edge by "making the model smaller", but by **replacing the inference stack**. vla.cpp uses a unified C++ runtime to cover 11 VLA architectures and shakes off the PyTorch dependency entirely, running all 200/200 episodes on an 8GB Jetson Orin Nano; vla.simd goes in the opposite hardware direction, using shared SIMD microkernels to get policies to 33.5 (fp32) / 81.2 (int8) actions/s on a GPU-less device like the **Raspberry Pi 5**; FoldQuantVLA keeps the quantization route but focuses on **the consistency of activation representations across calibration, rounding, and integer execution**, and honestly states the cost — language attention outputs and feed-forward down-projections must stay at 8 bits, about 1 ms extra on Orin. The common rule these three expose: **speed and fidelity cannot be accepted separately** (vla.cpp's "fixed-input checks must be accompanied by rollout evaluation" and FoldQuantVLA's "keeping 8 bits buys back 12.5 percentage points of success rate" are two sides of the same thing). (Source nature: all **Author's claim**, preprints)

**② A 3D rasterizer on ESP32-S3 also changes its license** —— [Jet](https://github.com/CubeCoders/Jet)

- **Analysis**: Jet is a dependency-free fixed-function 3D rasterizer for ESP32/STM32 (C++17). Three changes this time: triangle throughput on ESP32-S3 rises from about 40k to about 70k tris/sec (the author credits optimizations and SIMD tricks), screen-space reflection and distortion effects at 480×320 / 16bpp / 60fps, and the license changes from AGPL to **MIT**. Community reaction centers on the quality of the optimization, with some asking about the boundaries of shading and texture mapping. (Source nature: **Author's claim** (performance and optimization techniques) + **Measured** figures relayed by Hackaday; community assessments are subjective feedback)

**② "Decision models": a class of model that only outputs floating-point numbers** —— [Jev and decision models](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/21/jev/)

- **Analysis**: TypeSafe AI's Jev accepts text input but **outputs no text, only floating-point numbers** — corresponding to categories, yes/no, scores, and confidence, with questions evaluated in parallel (asking many takes about as long as asking one). Extremely cheap: billed on input only, output free, with the first model at $0.042 per million input tokens. The author thinks it suits classification-type tasks (spam, tagging, prioritization, search reranking — he measured BM25 fetching 100 candidates and having Jev score them), but also stresses that this is **a further regression toward black-box machine learning**: an LLM can at least give reasons, while Jev returns only a floating-point number, so **bias becomes harder to disentangle** and evaluation and structured experiments must come first. He also gives a vivid bias demo: having Jev score Bay Area cities as "good cities", Cupertino comes highest and East Palo Alto lowest. The official documentation also admits it is poor with numbers, dates, and adversarial content. (Source nature: **Author's claim** (third-party technical blog); pricing and behavior descriptions **To be verified**)
