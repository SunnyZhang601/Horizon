## 2. Deep Dives (Robotics · Embedded · EE/IC · AI)

### Robotics

**① "World models" used in three places at once this day: assembly, manipulation, and simulation** —— [Generalizable insertion assembly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28258) · [CoRe-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27314) · [PointCast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28393) · [InternW0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27656) · [Uranus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24815) · [Frozen latent flow losing motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28414)

- **Analysis**: These items point to the same thing — world models are shifting from "one model per task" toward "one model that consumes geometric diversity". The insertion-assembly item illustrates the mechanism best: instead of relying on task-specific policies, it feeds **proprioception and raw wrist vision** together into a single model, and after training on 90 geometries it gets 56% zero-shot on **never-seen shapes** versus only 7% for the model-free baseline; more crucial is the curve showing "performance keeps rising as the number of training-set objects grows" — that is **scalability**, not a single-point score bump. Pairs with it is the diagnostic value of the DART item: a frozen latent space + training only the flow **quietly loses motion**, and **evaluating with pixel error alone rewards static predictions** — that is, between a world model that "looks usable" and one that "is really predicting motion" you need dedicated criteria. Uranus and CoRe-WAM fill in from the two sides of "replacing hand-built modeling with data" and "temporal residual alignment".

**② On the contact and force side, today is dense enough to be its own section** —— [DexWrist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01008) · [Tactile sub-millimeter precision insertion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04649) · [TactileReflex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23568) · [LIFT force-feedback post-training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14236) · [VibeAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27344) · [GLoTouch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27695)

- **Analysis**: DexWrist is today's most information-dense hardware item — it gives the full spec sheet (rated torque 3.75 N·m, backdrivable torque 0.33 N·m, torque bandwidth 10.15 Hz, ±40°, 0.97 kg) and provides the decisive design point that **the velocity-constrained Jacobian is diagonal**: a one-to-one mapping from motors to DoF means no decoupling control is needed, which is precisely what lets it be evaluated directly as a "plug-and-play upgrade" on three existing arms. It goes together with the closing remark "stable contact without fine-tuned admittance control": **mechanical decoupling replaces control tuning**. The control side is another line — TactileReflex derives controller thresholds directly from the **inherent noise statistics of the sensors**, eliminating external force calibration and manual tuning, with three channels (shear magnitude S_y, contact force F_n, center of pressure C) driving prioritized reflexes at about 12 Hz; ablations show only the full three channels prevent irreversible container deformation (5/5 versus at most 1/5 for partial configurations).

**③ The "acceptance toolkit" of safety and benchmarks is expanding in step** —— [Industrial arm backdoor attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26868) · [HazardArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12447) · [HRC probabilistic safety guarantees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15221) · [ActiveArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24124) · [Pro-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27076) · [H2RBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24778)

- **Analysis**: Six items today belong to "testing and acceptance" rather than "capability gains", which is itself a signal. The industrial-arm backdoor item is the most unusual: it puts the attack surface on **real commercial arms (FANUC / xArm)** and measures the cost metrics of the defenses together (computational latency and execution overhead) — that is, safety is not "can it be defended" but "can it still run at high throughput after being defended". Pro-Bench offers a counter-intuitive finding: of 16 open-vocabulary model configurations, **10 perform best with short category labels and only 1 achieves the highest accuracy with free-form queries**, and **similar overall mAP can mask significant differences in object-retrieval consistency across paraphrases** — the same caution as DART's "overall metrics lie".

### Embedded

**① Neuromorphic computing lands on a US$5 MCU for the first time** —— [Flapping-wing robot SNN onboard closed-loop flight](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19430)

- **Analysis**: This is the only genuinely **onboard** result in today's embedded coverage, and its persuasiveness lies not in "SNNs work" but in the **comparison conditions**: on the same <30 g flapping-wing aircraft, the SNN controller cuts inference latency from 1059 μs to 680 μs (−36%) and power from 0.033 W to 0.027 W (−18%) versus a conventional ANN baseline, on an **ESP32**. These two numbers correspond to the two hardest items to concede within SWaP constraints, and it **does not depend on dedicated neuromorphic hardware** — overturning the default assumption that "spiking computing requires dedicated chips". Design-wise, the notable part is the layered structure: one SNN does state estimation, and the other **modulates the central pattern generator (CPG)** rather than outputting servo commands directly — the rhythm is delegated to a bio-inspired structure and the modulation to learning.

**② Edge GPU gains depend heavily on architecture, not algorithms** —— [GLASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28179) · [Onboard multi-fisheye ERP stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02319) · [BladeMaster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27342)

- **Analysis**: GLASS delivers today's sharpest set of numbers: the same library shows a **4.9× median and up to 81× difference between the best and worst placements**; 145 of 396 recommended placements differ between Jetson AGX Orin and RTX 5090; and the advantage over PyTorch / JAX is **73× on Orin but only 12× on RTX 5090**. The implication of this comparison is direct: **on the edge, the performance bottleneck is often the "placement decision" rather than the kernel itself**, so the same code must be re-tuned per machine, and the gains are largest on low-power hardware. There is also a byproduct worth remembering: when integrated into an already-published system it **found an existing numerical bug** and improved the embedded runtime by up to 1.5× — showing that the payoff of "rewriting the numerical stack" can exist independently of performance goals.

### AI

**① Video reasoning fills the "verifiable evaluation" gap** —— [VBVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20159)

- **Analysis**: Its value is not the dataset size (1 million clips, roughly three orders of magnitude beyond existing sets) but the **shift in scoring approach**: rule-based, human-aligned graders **replace model scoring**. Prior scaling research on video reasoning stalled precisely for lack of reproducible, interpretable evaluation; once the grader is rule-based, scale studies become meaningful. The authors' observed "early signs of emergent generalization to unseen reasoning tasks" is also only credible under rule-based scoring.

**② Coding agents expand from "writing code" to "manufacturing supervision data"** —— [EmbodiedSWE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27308) · [MCP layer connecting LLM reasoning and ROS navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27340) · [ART plug-and-play tool capability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14047)

- **Analysis**: The EmbodiedSWE chain deserves a separate look: a coding agent solves tasks in simulation → the verified solutions are **amplified into large amounts of diverse trajectories** → used to train VLAs, with observations that "the more generated demonstrations, the better the VLA" and "agent-assisted diversification improves generalization to held-out variants". It turns the agent's output from "an answer" into "**a scalable supervision signal**" — a usage completely different from "letting the agent directly control the robot". The MCP item goes the other way: it wraps the navigation representation layer (occupancy grids converted into images with metrics and poses, waypoint-level semantic annotation) into **standard MCP tools**, so any MCP-compatible LLM can use it without robot-specific wrappers, and **without modifying the existing ROS navigation stack** (map coverage >97% in simulated indoor environments).

**③ A diagnostic case that explains "metrics lie" thoroughly** —— [DART](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28414) · [VLA action-head initialization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13984)

- **Analysis**: Together the two form a pair. DART says **the loss function itself can reward wrong solutions** (pixel error alone rewards static predictions). The VLA action-head item is finer-grained: after sweeping action-head designs under controlled conditions with paired measured latencies, **the strongest single lever is copying the last few Transformer layers of the language backbone into the action head for initialization**, with no added latency; designs that "look stronger" such as flow matching and heavier decoders **help only when the action head is unaligned and reverse once aligned**; capacity pays off only after alignment, with gains falling off sharply near the scale of the existing π series. Most weighty is the authors' own wording: alignment is only **the interpretation that best organizes these measurements, not proven causation** — and they proactively state which controlled experiments would be needed to verify it.
