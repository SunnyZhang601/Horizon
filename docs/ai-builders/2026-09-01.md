**Peter Yang, AI tutorials creator**

If you are trying Fable 5.1, Peter Yang recommends running /claude-api prompt-audit on your skills, since it finds redundancies and rules the latest models no longer need. He pairs that with his skill hygiene philosophy: he keeps only about a dozen skills, mostly his own, regularly deletes the ones he no longer uses, and keeps every skill as short as possible. He also flags an open problem: when you iterate manually with AI and then ask it to fold the lessons back into the skill, the model often overfits on that one thread and the skill drifts over time.

Peter Yang 建议试用 Fable 5.1 时对自己的 skills 跑一遍 /claude-api prompt-audit,它会找出冗余和最新模型不再需要的规则。他还分享了自己的 skill 卫生习惯:只保留大约十几个 skills(大多是自己写的),定期删掉不再使用的,并尽量让每个 skill 保持简短。他也指出一个未解决的问题:当你手动迭代后再让 AI 把经验写回 skill,模型常常对那一次对话过拟合,时间久了 skill 就会漂移。

**Training Data: "Making Cities Awesome: Peregrine's Nick Noone & Ben Rudolph"**

The Takeaway: Peregrine's founders argue that the future of AI in government is not about collecting more surveillance data but about joining the data institutions already own, and that disciplined data governance is what makes powerful AI safe enough to deploy in policing.

Host Sonya talks with Nick Noone and Ben Rudolph, the founders of Peregrine, a data platform for public safety agencies. Noone's background is in forward deployed engineering, including work with US Special Operations in the Middle East, while Rudolph spent years on refugee response at the UNHCR before building tuberculosis drug adherence apps with the Indian government in rural India. That shaped a contrarian thesis: safety is the bottom of the pyramid for "how to make cities awesome." The company was born in early 2018 after more than two dozen rejections, when a cold call to a police commander led to a first ride-along and, eventually, real access.

Their business model inverts the data collection playbook of companies like Flock and Axon. "We're fundamentally in the business of joining disparate information," Noone says, and the customer always owns its data, with granular permission controls and sovereignty built in. That anti network effect positioning is deliberate. The payoff is concrete: a Florida county traced more than 100 water rescues to a three day weather pattern that created rip currents, and a detective used semantic search to surface a pattern of antisemitic threats against synagogues.

The engineering story is just as sharp. Rudolph estimates that "95% of the work is what happens before the user types in the question," so roughly half of Peregrine's engineers work on the data platform, and about 90% of the Python notebook data integrations are now written by agents, with deployment teams overseeing long running orchestrators and sub-agents. Their first agent reproduced a cold case exoneration result, and in one Wisconsin county, cell records mined from 300GB of data placed a suspect at the location where a body was found. On facial recognition, their stance is pragmatic: do not impose Silicon Valley red lines on customers who are accountable to their own communities.

本期要点:Peregrine 的创始人认为,AI 在政府领域的未来不在于收集更多监控数据,而在于把机构已经拥有的数据整合起来;严格的数据治理,才让强大的 AI 能安全地部署到警务工作中。

主持人 Sonya 与公共安全数据平台 Peregrine 的两位创始人 Nick Noone 和 Ben Rudolph 对谈。Noone 的背景是前置部署工程,包括与美军特种部队在中东的合作;Rudolph 则曾在联合国难民署多年从事难民救援工作,后来在印度乡村与印度政府合作开发肺结核服药依从性应用。这些经历塑造了一个反主流的判断:安全是"如何让城市变得更好"这座金字塔的底层。公司诞生于 2018 年初,在那之前他们被拒绝了二十多次,一通打给一位警队指挥官的陌生电话带来了第一次随车巡逻,最终换来了真正的系统访问权限。

他们的商业模式与 Flock、Axon 这类数据收集公司的打法正好相反。Noone 说:"We're fundamentally in the business of joining disparate information"(我们本质上做的是把分散的信息连接起来)。客户始终拥有自己的数据,细粒度的权限控制和数据主权是内置的。这种反网络效应的定位是有意为之。回报非常具体:佛罗里达州的一个县把 100 多起水上救援追溯到一个持续三天的天气模式,它制造了离岸流;一位侦探用语义搜索发现了针对犹太教堂的反犹威胁模式。

工程侧的故事同样精彩。Rudolph 估计"95% 的工作发生在用户输入问题之前",因此 Peregrine 大约一半的工程师在做数据平台,约 90% 的 Python notebook 数据集成如今由 agent 编写,部署团队负责监督长时间运行的 orchestrator 与 sub-agent。他们的第一个 agent 复现了一桩悬案的平反结论;在威斯康星州的一个县,从 300GB 数据中挖掘出的通话记录,把一名嫌疑人定位在发现尸体的地点。关于人脸识别,他们的态度很务实:不要把硅谷的红线强加给那些要对自己社区负责的客户。
