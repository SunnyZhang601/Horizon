**Dan Shipper, CEO of Every**

Dan Shipper critiqued a widely shared AI-and-jobs report, naming two assumptions that change the analysis. First, decomposing jobs into tasks is useful but incomplete, and under that model it is unclear where new tasks come from. Second, the report models human tasks created by automation as only a fraction of the tasks automated, while in his experience automation often creates multiples more work for humans. Drawing on Wittgenstein and Heidegger, he argued a job is a way of seeing and caring about the world: what you care about shapes what you notice, so as tools change, the same cares generate new tasks.

Dan Shipper（Every CEO）点评了一份广为流传的 AI 与就业报告，指出两个足以改变整个分析的假设。第一，把工作拆解成任务（tasks）虽然有用但不完整，照这个模型，新任务从哪里来并不清楚。第二，报告把自动化给人类创造的新任务建模成仅占被自动化任务的一小部分，而根据他的经验，自动化常常给人类带来数倍的新工作。他借 Wittgenstein 和 Heidegger 的视角提出：一份工作是一种看待世界、在乎世界的方式；你在乎什么决定了你注意到什么，所以工具一变，同样的在乎就会催生新的任务。
