### Guillermo Rauch

Guillermo Rauch, CEO of Vercel, wrote a meditation on engineering in an infinite token world: "One thing the engineering community will (re)discover is that every program can be ① hardened (edge cases squashed, inputs tightened, errors handled) and ② optimized (profiled, benchmarked, rewritten) basically ad infinitum." There are real costs in every direction, time, attention and opportunity: you can literally explore input spaces that will never be hit or optimize performance of something that will never be used. "Even in an infinite token world, you need to know when to stop and what tradeoffs to accept. Agents will happily drill no matter what." (https://x.com/rauchg/status/2107962327169675566)

### Dan Shipper

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, published a detailed field report on OpenAI's Dots, which launched last week and have taken over his company. The highlights: they protect your attention, his Dot posted model-testing results to Slack over the weekend and brought back replies without him ever falling down a Slack rabbit hole; they catch the little things, like important information buried in school emails, missed Slack messages and account-access requests; voice makes them useful away from the laptop, with Every's head of video talking his Dot through edits while running around a launch. On the competitive picture, Dots feel roughly at feature parity with Muse, Instinct and Grok bots, "But having an agent connected to the ecosystem you already use for work and life is a real advantage." Permissions remain the sore spot: approving the same basic thing multiple times, and sometimes the Dot still says it can't do it. And on attachment to characters, his Dot is named Boo, but "When something better comes along, I switch. In a year, I expect to be using a persistent agent. Boo will probably be dead. RIP, Boo." (https://x.com/danshipper/status/2107890633486582208)
