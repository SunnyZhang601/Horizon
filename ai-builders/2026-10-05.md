### Guillermo Rauch

Guillermo Rauch, CEO of Vercel, introduced gdp-ts: "Ghosts of Departed Proofs for TypeScript", a library, linter and AI skill for safer API design. Under this contract, sensitive functions require proofs that the caller performed an authorization check, and the typechecker verifies those proofs at compile time, preventing your team and agents from shipping catastrophic security bugs. The timing twist is what makes it interesting: these patterns existed for years in ecosystems like Haskell, but human code review and cognitive overhead kept them niche. "The situation is now inverted. Agents are writing more code than we can review, and they *thrive* in tight loops with hard constraints that would frustrate us." The README models a real Vercel API constraint: changing a project's password requires a proof of a certain role plus a certain entitlement (https://x.com/rauchg/status/2107119811444748555)。

### Aaron Levie

Aaron Levie, CEO of Box, argued that a decent amount of agent adoption is bottlenecked by the ability to test, tune and optimize agents on real work environments: the files they need, the CRM environments, email and more. It is impossible to know how agents are performing without understanding how well they execute on your evals, and every enterprise has to do this one by one right now. His conclusion: "Every enterprise is going to have someone managing evals, infrastructure for building and running evals and simulated environments, and more. Huge space" (https://x.com/levie/status/2107283615247999257)。

### Ryo Lu

Ryo Lu, a designer who has worked on Cursor, Notion and Stripe, published a moving remembrance of Steve Jobs: growing up with beige boxes, then seeing the iMac G4 in Canada, and being unable to afford a Mac so he made his PC look like one, changing icons, fonts and windows and learning to draw shiny lickable buttons, which is how he got into interface design. He burned DVDs of Steve's keynotes and studied them again and again. Since Jobs died he has felt the industry stuck in a loop of constant refinement, better supply chains, and more ways to keep people scrolling, so much talent spent on tiny improvements with so little room for something truly great and heartfelt.
