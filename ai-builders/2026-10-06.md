### Ep 94: Applied Compute CEO on the Limits of RL, the New AI Hyperscaler & Why Post-Training Wins Inference

https://www.youtube.com/@RedpointAI

The Takeaway: Josh, CEO of Applied Compute, joined the Unsupervised Learning podcast to argue that the next phase of AI competition is about who owns their own intelligence, by which he means control of the post-training stack rather than raw model access. His core worry is dependency: models can get pulled away from you, especially in coding, and the OpenAI and Base10 partnership is to him a signal that a multi-model future is coming, where companies will not bet everything on one lab. He framed the economics with a Pareto curve: "if I'm able to take a cheaper model and train it to be parity with the frontier but at much better cost, sometimes you're able to serve more users." On the technical path, he described a pipeline that goes from out-of-distribution data to judgment traces to offline RL and finally online RL, calling reinforcement learning "a hill climbing machine. The hardest part is actually defining the hill to climb, which is why evals... is a really important thing for companies to A, focus on building and B) actually safeguard pretty carefully." His advice: keep your evals private, and treat training data and evals the same way, as task plus environment plus verifier. He walked through a Harvey case study where post-training produced roughly a 10 percent token efficiency cut on a review-table task, and noted "you'd be surprised how underutilized GPUs are," with the Jevons paradox very much real. He closed by describing the new AI hyperscaler as an inverted pyramid: training at the narrow bottom, then inference, then routing, and finally the widest layer, the context and harness work where most value now accrues.

核心要点：Applied Compute 的 CEO Josh 在 Unsupervised Learning 播客上提出，AI 竞争的下一阶段看的是谁"own your intelligence"（真正拥有自己的智能），也就是控制 post-training 技术栈，而不是拿到模型访问权就够了。他最担心的是依赖：模型可能被"收回"，coding 领域尤其如此；OpenAI 与 Base10 的合作在他看来是多模型未来的信号，企业不会再把宝全押在一家实验室身上。他用 Pareto 曲线讲经济账："if I'm able to take a cheaper model and train it to be parity with the frontier but at much better cost, sometimes you're able to serve more users"（用更便宜的模型训到与 frontier 打平、成本又低得多，有时反而能服务更多用户）。技术路径上他描述了一条从 OOD 数据到 judgment traces、再到 offline RL、最后 online RL 的流水线，并把 RL 叫做 "a hill climbing machine. The hardest part is actually defining the hill to climb, which is why evals... is a really important thing for companies to A, focus on building and B) actually safeguard pretty carefully"（RL 就是爬坡机，最难的是定义爬哪座坡，所以 evals 既是企业要重点建设的，也是要严加保护的资产）。他的建议是：evals 要保密，训练数据和 eval 要用同一种方式对待，即 task 加 environment 加 verifier。他讲了 Harvey 的案例：post-training 在一个 review-table 任务上把 token 效率提升了约 10%，还提醒大家 "you'd be surprised how underutilized GPUs are"（GPU 的利用率低得会让你吃惊），Jevons 悖论真实存在。最后他把新型 AI hyperscaler 描绘成一座倒金字塔：最窄的底层是 training，往上是 inference、routing，最宽的一层是 context 和 harness 的工作，如今大部分价值都在这里沉淀。

### Google's AI Infrastructure Chief, Amin Vahdat, on the Physics & Economics of Frontier AI

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLOhHNjZItNnMm5tdW61JpnyxeYH5NDDx8

The Takeaway: Amin Vahdat, Google's chief AI infrastructure officer, joined the Training Data podcast to give the most candid account yet of what it takes to run frontier AI at Google's scale. He put a number on the moment: more than 200 billion dollars of CapEx, "the biggest CapEx build out in human history." His favorite metric provocation: "FLOPS is a vanity metric," and what matters instead is goodput, useful work per second. Systems at 100,000 accelerator scale fail multiple times a day, so resilience is a design requirement, not an add-on. Serving capacity doubles roughly every six months, and "as much or more of that is going to come from software as it is from hardware." He told the contrarian history of the TPU: started in 2013 against the prevailing wisdom that "specialization never wins," and the transformer's arrival "completely shifted the TPU program," with this year's parts carrying 8 times the inference and 8 times the training capability. Co-design only works because Google can intercept tape-outs, something he called "somewhere between hard and impossible" across company boundaries. On agents, he warned that "there's no human in the loop that is going to naturally rate limit how quickly requests are going to go to the model," which changes the physics of serving entirely. Power is "the single most fundamental constraint," and Google's optical circuit switch redirects light to a spare rack in milliseconds; 7 to 8 year old TPUs still run at 100 percent utilization, software like TorchTPU keeps old silicon valuable, and on orbital data centers he sees "no showstoppers here."

核心要点：Google 的 AI 基础设施负责人 Amin Vahdat 在 Training Data 播客上给出了迄今最坦率的一线讲述。他先给这个时代标了价：超过 2000 亿美元的 CapEx，"biggest CapEx build out in human history"（人类历史上最大的资本开支建设）。他最喜欢的一句挑战认知的话是 "FLOPS is a vanity metric"：FLOPS 只是虚荣指标，真正重要的是 goodput，即每秒完成的有用工作。10 万加速器规模的系统一天要挂好几次，所以韧性是设计前提而非附加项。serving 容量大约每 6 个月翻一倍，而且 "as much or more of that is going to come from software as it is from hardware"（其中来自软件的部分不会少于来自硬件的部分）。他讲了 TPU 的逆向操作史：2013 年立项时主流共识是 "specialization never wins"（专用化从来不赢），而 transformer 的出现 "completely shifted the TPU program"，今年这代芯片已经做到 8I 加 8T，推理和训练能力各翻 8 倍。软硬件 co-design 之所以可行，是因为 Google 能在中途拦截 tape-out，他形容跨公司边界做这件事 "somewhere between hard and impossible"（介于非常难和不可能之间）。谈到 agent，他警告 "there's no human in the loop that is going to naturally rate limit how quickly requests are going to go to the model"：agent 时代没有人类在环里天然地限流，serving 的物理形态将彻底改变。而 "power is the single most fundamental constraint"，Google 的 OCS 光路交换能在毫秒级把流量切到备用机架；七八年前的老 TPU 依然跑在 100% 利用率上，TorchTPU 这类软件让旧硅片持续保值；至于轨道数据中心，他的判断是 "no showstoppers here"，没有发现根本性的拦路虎。
