### Madhu Guru

Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta who previously led Gemini, Veo and Nano Banana at Google, mapped the startup opportunity hiding in plain sight: "every layer of the computing stack will need to be rebuilt for agents." The last few years focused on making enterprise software agent-ready, APIs, connectors and MCPs, and now personal agents are forcing the same conversation for consumer products, with e-commerce an early example. His checklist: desktop and mobile OSes where agents are first-class users, cloud infra built for agents to operate directly, identity, permissions and security designed for thousands of agents acting on our behalf, and interfaces designed for agent and human users working together. "If you're looking for startup ideas, go through each layer of the computing stack and ask: what has to change when the primary user is an agent?" ### Nikunj Kothari

Nikunj Kothari, partner at FPV Ventures, wrote the hard-truth playbook for seed founders whose models have not caught up yet: he has the same conversation at least twice a week, close to 1 million in revenue, stellar customers, dwindling runway, founders feeling the opportunity cost. First, "if you have intense belief in the company, be a cockroach and get to default profitable. once the capabilities get there, you feel the demand, then go raise a large round and go for the jugular." Second, find an orthogonal area growing like crazy and ride the coat tails of new capabilities with unfair advantages. Third, sell or get acquihired, join a more ambitious company, and start fresh one or two years later. "The bar continues to get harder every month on how to cross the chasm from seed -> series A. ### Why Every Traded Personal Agents for One Company Agent

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuMcoKK9mKgHtW_o9h5sGO2vXrffKHwJL

The Takeaway: Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, sat down with Willie Williams, the company's head of platform, on the AI & I podcast to explain why Every scrapped the dream of giving every employee a personal agent and built a single company agent instead. The backstory is pure 2026: when they first let agents loose, Every's Slack filled with bots making a mess, "so many bots in our Slack, like absolute chaos." Willie's insight was to flip the investment model: "instead of everyone investing in their own agents... why don't we take all that energy and put it into one agent that the whole company benefits from?" On the professional side he is now convinced it is one agent per company or org structure, and "I really don't see us or the industry repeating the chaos of every worker gets their coworker," while on the personal side personalization still wins. He offered a manager's definition worth keeping: leverage is "how much of the field you can see." The practical lesson from Every's rollout is that agents excel at work that is "really definable and really testable," like ops dashboards, anomaly detection routed every five minutes, and first-response on-call, and Willie showed how his own token usage would "fall off a cliff" on ambiguous tasks because "when it comes to action, there's no billion token move I can make." Dan closed with a prediction: "I would expect in a year, this is the default way that you're interacting with all your AIs," and then, in the same breath, an epitaph for his own Dot: "Boo will probably be dead. RIP, Boo."
