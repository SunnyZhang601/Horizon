**Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta, previously led Gemini, Veo and Nano Banana at Google**

Madhu Guru argues every AI product should abstract model choice away from users, because the intelligence is just the engine and all the user cares about is that the job gets done. Making that work takes three things: deep understanding of the model frontier across different weight classes for your use cases, great evals for your workflows so you know which models work best where, and a team with the talent and an AI native operating model to keep adapting as the frontier moves. Having worked with the Lovable team, he says they have all three.

Madhu Guru 认为,所有 AI 产品都应该把"选模型"这件事从用户面前抽象掉:智能只是底层引擎,用户只关心任务有没有完成。要做到这一点需要三件事:针对你的 use case 深刻理解不同 weight class 的模型前沿;为你的 workflow 建立优秀的 evals,知道哪个模型在哪最合适;以及一支有人才密度和 AI 原生运营模式的团队,能随前沿移动持续调整。他与 Lovable 团队合作过,称他们三条全占。

**AI & I by Every: "How a Professional Writer Writes With AI"**

The Takeaway: A professional writer who was once an AI skeptic now runs her whole writing business, and much of her life, through AI agents, and her biggest lesson is that the model was never the hard part: supplying fresh, specific context is.

On AI & I, host Natalia Quintero talks with Katie Parrott, a staff writer at Every, about her experimental Compound Writing plugin, inspired by Kieran Klaassen's Compound Engineering. Parrott's arc is striking: laid off from a crypto firm where she worked as an editor and ghostwriter, she replaced a $150 an hour human career coach with a $20 a month ChatGPT subscription and never looked back. "One of my favorite things about writing is that I never know what I think until I write it down, but once I write it down it becomes obvious," she writes, yet clarity, she adds, "doesn't arrive gift wrapped from a digital assistant."

The results speak for themselves: eight blog posts, three ebooks, 24 LinkedIn posts, 24 X posts and 16 Instagram posts in two weeks, work she describes as becoming "a content agency of one." Her context engineering started early, with style guide style context files on brand messaging and audience pasted manually before Projects existed. Her mental model is a kitchen: the model is the kitchen, outlining is chopping, composition is boiling, but the ingredients must be fresh, because what the model already knows is commoditized information. The real leverage is the last mile of human knowledge past the knowledge cutoff.

The plugin structures writing into brainstorming, outlining, drafting and staged edits, with reviewer skills modeled on Vonnegut's story structure and Hitchcock's suspense. She calls it "a gym... a workout system to make you a better writer" rather than a replacement. Beyond writing, AI is supportive technology for managing her bipolar disorder: Codex booked a primary care appointment she had postponed for three years, and her career coach is now a Codex project with a dossier, OKRs and an AI maintained Kanban board. Her closing worry is access: the risk that AI's value "compounds to this small subset of people that happen to be early," when a more compelling future is one where everyone comes along for the ride.
