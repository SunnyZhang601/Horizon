## 二、领域深读（机器人 · 嵌入式 · EE/IC · AI）

### 机器人

- **TinySDP：把「非凸约束下的实时优化」搬到 MCU 上。** 它的真正贡献不是「又一个 MPC」，而是在**资源受限的嵌入式目标上把半定规划做成实时可解**——用缓存 Riccati 结构吃 ADMM 的计算结构、再用 rank-1 证书把松弛解升回可用的几何保证。也就是说它同时解决了两件事：算力不够，以及非线性松弛解不可信。[来源](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13748)
  - ⚠️ 注意边界：验证平台是 Crazyflie 四旋翼，属学术验证；论文 73% 的路径缩短是与特定基线对比的结果，不能直接当性能承诺。

- **灵巧手四条串读（移动 · 容错 · 触觉 · 抓取）**：今日 arXiv 里灵巧手/手部方向同时出现了四条互补工作，值得合起来看——
  - **拟人手指尖行走**（ETH）：同一只 20 自由度全直驱手，既当腿又当手，任务专用 RL 策略 + 板载树莓派。[来源](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17172)
  - **并发式 [残差故障自适应 RFA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17404)**：针对「运行时隐藏的命令通道关节故障」，保留一个冻结的健康教师策略提供标称行为，再训一个循环残差策略从本体感觉与命令响应史中推断纠正动作；关键工程手段是**故障注入域随机化（FIDR）**——随机化故障模式、受影响关节、严重程度、发生时间，并对近期表现差的模式加权采样。部署时控制器**不接收故障标签、不做控制器切换**，靠泛化兜住。
  - **触觉采集**：[XRoboToolKit-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16437) 指出行业痛点——现有遥操作数据采集**拿不到稳定高频的触觉反馈**，导致接触密集任务的数据质量受限。
  - **腕带式力感知**：[柔性电容腕带](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16518) 把传感器从手上挪到手腕，用肌肉/肌腱产生的压力模式反推全手姿态与接触力；单指 R²=0.57 说明「纯腕部信号估力」目前仍是中等精度，需外部姿态信号补强。

- **TARC：控制频率本身也是可学习的变量。** 定频控制器有个默认假设——为保证鲁棒性，控制频率往高取。TARC 让策略连「这个动作要持续多久」一起输出，在切换次数约束下优化，结果控制频率不到定频方案一半而性能相当。[来源](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23176)

- **逃逸感知 CBF：安全认证里的「来得及」问题。** 传统只依赖状态的屏障会认证那些「状态看着安全、但受角速率限制根本来不及减速」的情况；论文给出这个认证缺口宽度的闭式刻画（∝ 接近速度，∝ 1/角速率上限），并把屏障定义在「状态 + 上一时刻输入」的增广对上。[来源](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17292)

- **边角补充**：掌上双桨水下机器人（65mm / 34g，两桨垂直堆叠，PID + 差动推力俯仰、尾舵偏航，游速 13.9 体长/秒、跃出水面 1.6 体长高）[来源](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17240)——它把「差动推力替代控制舵面」做得很干净，可当控制分配的思路参考。另有 [ConGraspXL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16319)（抓取约束的组合式可控合成：接近方向 / 接触区域 / 手腕轨迹 / 功能手姿，用掩码残差接口组合约束），属学术向，暂无落地证据。

1. **[TinySDP: a real-time semidefinite programming solver for embedded robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13748)** (⭐️ 8.0/10, among the highest scores of this window) — The first semidefinite programming (SDP) solver designed specifically for embedded systems, letting microcontrollers run real-time MPC on problems **with non-convex obstacle constraints**: it embeds the positive-semidefinite cone projection into a cache-based Riccati-ADMM solver, paired with a posterior rank-1 certificate that converts the relaxed solution into explicit geometric guarantees. Paths are up to 73% shorter than the strongest baseline on dead-end and dynamic obstacle avoidance benchmarks; real-time performance already validated on a Crazyflie quadrotor.

10. **[XRoboToolKit-T: haptic force-controlled teleoperation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16437)** (⭐️ 7.0/10) — Uses "a stabilizer haptic module to rapidly analyze normal force distribution and infer pseudo-shear forces" for real-time haptic assistance, then uses "a refiner module integrating a VLA" to refine actions based on haptic data and task descriptions; validated on contact-rich tasks such as grasping a deformable rubber pipette for liquid transfer and inserting a syringe into a vascular training pad. ⚠️ Single preprint, abstract truncated, no peer review or benchmark comparisons.
11. **[Wristband pressure sensor array enables whole-hand pose and contact force estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16518)** (⭐️ 7.0/10) — A flexible capacitive array wrapped around the wrist + an RNN infers whole-hand pose and distributed contact forces from pressure patterns generated by muscle contractions and tendon displacement, **with no electrical contact with the skin and no sensors worn on the hand**. Mean finger-joint MAE of 4.6° for single-user isolated movements; per-finger contact force R²=0.57 with 4 users manipulating everyday objects, rising to 0.75 when an external pose signal is introduced.
12. **[A new quadrotor safety method: escape-aware control barrier functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17292)** (⭐️ 7.0/10) — Points out that CBFs for input-constrained systems almost always "depend only on state", which on a quadrotor certifies states that **in fact leave no time to escape**: the width of this certification gap is proportional to approach speed and inversely proportional to the maximum achievable body angular rate. The authors define an escape barrier on the augmented pair of "state + previous input", give a closed-form expression and an analytical inverse for the maximum certifiable approach speed, and it can be embedded in predictive controllers without adding state dimensions. All 550 closed-loop experiments passed, while the compared stopping-distance barrier failed 15% / 25% in the two scenario classes. ⚠️ 13-state simulation only, no real hardware.

## 2. Deep Dives (Robotics · Embedded · EE/IC · AI)

### Robotics

- **TinySDP: bringing "real-time optimization under non-convex constraints" onto MCUs.** Its real contribution is not "yet another MPC" but making semidefinite programming real-time solvable **on resource-constrained embedded targets** — using cached Riccati structure to exploit ADMM's computational structure, then using a rank-1 certificate to lift the relaxed solution back to usable geometric guarantees. In other words it solves two problems at once: insufficient compute, and nonlinear relaxed solutions being untrustworthy. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13748)
  - ⚠️ Note the boundary: the validation platform is a Crazyflie quadrotor, which is academic validation; the paper's 73% path shortening is a result against a specific baseline and cannot be taken directly as a performance promise.

- **A four-item dexterous-hand thread (locomotion · fault tolerance · touch · grasping)**: four complementary dexterous-hand/hand-related works appeared simultaneously in today's arXiv, worth reading together —
  - **Fingertip walking for a humanoid hand** (ETH): the same 20-DoF fully direct-driven hand serves as both leg and hand, with a task-specific RL policy + onboard Raspberry Pi. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17172)
  - **Concurrent [residual fault-adaptive RFA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17404)**: targeting "command-channel joint faults hidden at runtime", it retains a frozen healthy teacher policy to provide nominal behavior and trains a recurrent residual policy to infer corrective actions from proprioception and command-response history; the key engineering device is **fault-injection domain randomization (FIDR)** — randomizing fault mode, affected joints, severity, and onset time, with weighted sampling of recently poorly-performing modes. At deployment the controller **receives no fault labels and performs no controller switching**, relying on generalization to hold up.
  - **Tactile data collection**: [XRoboToolKit-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16437) points out an industry pain point — existing teleoperation data collection **cannot obtain stable high-frequency haptic feedback**, limiting data quality for contact-rich tasks.
  - **Wristband-based force sensing**: the [flexible capacitive wristband](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16518) moves the sensor from the hand to the wrist, inferring whole-hand pose and contact forces from pressure patterns produced by muscles/tendons; per-finger R²=0.57 shows that "force estimation from wrist signals alone" is still only medium accuracy for now and needs external pose signals to reinforce it.

- **TARC: control frequency itself is a learnable variable.** Fixed-rate controllers carry a default assumption — for robustness, set the control frequency high. TARC lets the policy output "how long this action should last" together with the action, optimizing under a switch-count constraint; the result is comparable performance at less than half the fixed-rate control frequency. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23176)

- **Escape-aware CBF: the "is there still time" problem in safety certification.** Traditional state-only barriers certify cases that "look safe in state but physically cannot decelerate in time due to angular rate limits"; the paper gives a closed-form characterization of the width of this certification gap (∝ approach speed, ∝ 1/angular-rate limit) and defines the barrier on the augmented pair of "state + previous input". [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17292)

- **Corner-case additions**: a palm-sized dual-propeller underwater robot (65mm / 34g, two propellers stacked vertically, PID + differential thrust for pitch, rudder for yaw, swim speed 13.9 body lengths/second, leap-out-of-water 1.6 body lengths high) [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17240) — it executes "differential thrust replacing control surfaces" very cleanly, usable as a control-allocation idea reference. There is also [ConGraspXL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16319) (compositional controllable synthesis of grasping constraints: approach direction / contact region / wrist trajectory / functional hand pose, combining constraints via a masked residual interface), academic in nature with no deployment evidence for now.

### Embedded / Hardware

- **The Flock camera incident: a textbook counterexample of hardware product security.** What matters is the **nature** of the vulnerability, not the count — **hardcoded API keys**, and **credentials stored in plaintext**. Once devices are deployed in unsupervised public spaces, the threat model must include "local physical access"; one commenter's judgment was harsher: assembling a product from off-the-shelf hardware/software stacks makes a widened attack surface an almost inevitable outcome. [Source](https://www.wired.com/story/hackers-flock-camera-data-shows-how-system-works/) ([Community discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49726586))

### EE / IC

Technically it attaches more DRAM dies to the same module (RDIMMs trade registered buffering of address/control signals for capacity); the real engineering questions are cost and power — the current 256GB DDR5-6400 runs about USD 19,000, and the 512GB price will raise the adoption bar significantly. ### AI

- **[4B model generates query plans](https://rohanbansal.com/qorl): a "small model + distillation" route, and an argument about benchmarks.** The method trains a 4B model to replace Postgres's heuristic optimizer, claimed to produce plans 81% faster, distilled from a larger model. Community objections center on the test conditions (in-memory dataset, restricted buffers, warmed-up queries, read-only workload) and overfitting risk; some argue LLMs are a "blunt instrument" in this scenario and would rather see AlphaGo-style neural heuristic methods. [Community discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49731285)

- **[Mistral × Mozilla](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-x-mozilla/): the gap between the privacy narrative and architectural transparency.** The feature itself is not new (compare Chrome's Gemini Nano); the point of contention is that the official announcement **did not make clear the boundary between local inference and cloud inference**, leading the community to question whether "zero data retention" is verifiable. [Community discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49723408)

- **[Dream-RSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14858): another step in the world-model direction, but the naming was questioned.** Multiple agents competitively improve task performance within a limited number of refinement steps, and a historical-replay simulator is used for off-policy evaluation to avoid expensive rollouts; several commenters pointed out this looks more like an optimization of existing training than a system capable of permanent self-improvement, and it clearly builds on Danijar Hafner's Dreamer series. [Community discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49726955)

- **[World-action-model survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16074)**: organizes the boundaries, taxonomy, and evaluation protocols of WAMs into a citable map, and highlights five open challenges (action alignment, world-action factorization, spatial and multi-view consistency, long-horizon memory, neural simulation for closed-loop policy learning, and efficient inference). [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16074)
